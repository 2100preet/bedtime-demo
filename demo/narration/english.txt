The house had gone quiet in the way houses do when the day is finished.

{child} sat on the rug with both feet tucked in, and {narrator} sat close enough that their shoulders touched. Outside, a scooter went by and then the lane was still. On the windowsill there was a tiny clay lamp, no bigger than a fist, with a cotton wick curled like a sleeping mouse.

"Shall we light it?" {narrator} asked.

{child} nodded, very serious, the way small people are when a job matters.

{narrator} struck a match. The flame was shy at first. It leaned, found the wick, and stood up gold. The room did not become bright. It became kind. Shadows stayed in the corners and did not bother anyone.

"This lamp is not for the whole city," {narrator} said. "It is for this room, and for you. When the day has been loud, a small light is enough."

They watched it together. The flame nodded left, then right, as if it were listening.

{child} told the lamp about the day. There had been a mango that was almost too sweet, and a friend who shared a crayon. The lamp did not fix anything. It only stayed, the way a friend stays when there is nothing clever to say.

{narrator} warmed a little milk, with honey stirred until the spoon went quiet. {child} drank it slowly. Warmth walked down to the toes and decided to stay there.

"What does the moon do when we sleep?" {child} asked.

"It keeps the lane," {narrator} said. "And I keep this side of the blanket. You do not have to keep anything tonight. Not the toys, not the worries, not the last bit of the day."

The razai came up, soft and a little heavy, the good kind of heavy. {narrator} tucked it under a small chin and smoothed the wrinkle near one ear. A hand rested on the blanket, warm and sure, counting nothing at all.

"If you wake, the lamp will still be a friend, and I will still be here," {narrator} said. "You can close your eyes. The story can finish itself."

{child} tried one eye, then the other. The flame made a tiny sound, like a page turning. Somewhere a dog settled on a step. Somewhere a fan turned, round and round, in no hurry to be finished.

"Good night, {child}," {narrator} whispered. "The light stays in your heart, even when the lamp is quiet. I am right here."

And the room held still, and the night was gentle, and {child} slept with the small gold flame keeping watch until morning could come slowly.
