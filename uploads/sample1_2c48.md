# Aarav and Dadi's Diwali Diya
*A Lorikins sample story for Aarav, age 4, narrated by Dadi*

It was Diwali night, and the whole house was glowing.

Aarav sat on the soft rug next to his Dadi. Dadi is Papa's mummy, and she smelled like cardamom and warm milk. In her hands she held a tiny clay lamp. "Dekho, Aarav," she said. *Look.* "This is a *diya*. Tonight we light lots and lots of diyas, so the whole world feels bright and happy."

Aarav touched the little lamp very gently. It was smooth and round, like a tiny boat.

"Why do we light them, Dadi?" he asked.

Dadi smiled her crinkly smile. "Long, long ago, Lord Ram came home after a very long journey. Everyone was so *khush*, so happy, that they lit diyas all along the path, so he could find his way home in the dark. Now every Diwali, we light diyas to say: welcome home, light. Welcome home, love."

Aarav thought about that. "Like when Papa leaves the hallway light on for me?"

"Bilkul," said Dadi. *Exactly.* "Just like that."

Together they walked to the window. Outside, the neighbours' houses twinkled. There were strings of lights like little stars, and a big *rangoli* on the doorstep, a flower drawn with bright powder, pink and yellow and blue.

Dadi gave Aarav one small *laddoo*, round and golden and sweet. "One for you," she whispered, "and one for the moon." She pretended to toss a crumb up to the sky. Aarav giggled.

Then Dadi lit the diya. The flame was small and gold, and it danced a little, swaying side to side. "Ssh," said Dadi. "The flame is sleepy too. See how slowly it moves?"

Aarav watched. The light went left... and right... left... and right. His eyes went left... and right... and felt a little heavy.

Far away, a firework went *phat-phat-phat* and turned into glittering flowers in the sky. Then it was quiet again.

"Dadi," Aarav yawned, "does the diya stay on all night?"

"For a little while," said Dadi, tucking his *razai*, his cosy blanket, up to his chin. "And when it goes out, the light stays inside you. In here." She tapped his heart softly, once, twice. "Wherever you go, Aarav, you carry the Diwali light."

Aarav put his hand on his heart. It felt warm, like the little lamp.

Outside, the stars came out one by one, as if the sky was lighting its own diyas. Dadi hummed a soft *lori*, a lullaby she used to sing to Papa when he was small.

"Shubh Diwali, mera raja beta," she whispered. *Happy Diwali, my precious boy.*

"Shubh Diwali, Dadi," Aarav murmured.

And with the little diya glowing gold, and Dadi's hand on his hair, Aarav floated softly, slowly, sweetly off to sleep.

*Shubh ratri, Aarav. Good night.*
