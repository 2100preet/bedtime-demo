# Meera and Nani's Rainbow Rain
*A Lorikins sample story for Meera, age 3, narrated by Nani*

*Tip, tip, tip.* Rain was tapping on the window.

Meera pressed her nose against the glass. "Nani, *baarish*!" she said. *Rain!*

Nani laughed and came to sit beside her. Nani is Mummy's mummy, and her shawl was soft as a cloud. "Haan, Meera. In India, when lots and lots of rain comes, we call it the *monsoon*. The sky gets so full of water that it has to share it with everyone: the trees, the flowers, the frogs, and little girls named Meera."

Outside, a frog said *tarr-tarr*. Meera giggled. "The frog is happy!"

"Very happy," said Nani. "And the peacock too. When the monsoon comes, the *mor*, the peacock, opens his big blue-green feathers and dances in the rain."

Meera stood up and spread her arms wide like peacock feathers. She turned around slowly, once, twice. Nani clapped softly.

Then Nani made two cups of warm *haldi doodh*, golden milk, with a little honey. "For when it's rainy and cosy," she said.

Meera sipped. It was warm all the way down to her toes.

"Nani," Meera asked, "what comes after the rain?"

Nani pointed. The rain was getting slower... *tip... tip...* and through the clouds came a little bit of sun. And there, across the sky, was a rainbow.

"Look, Meera. The sky is playing *Holi*!"

"What's Holi?" asked Meera.

"Holi is the festival of colours," said Nani. "In spring, everyone throws soft coloured powder, called *gulaal*, in the air. Pink, and green, and yellow, and blue. Everybody laughs, and hugs, and says '*Holi hai!*' And for one day, everybody is the same colour, all rainbow, all friends."

Meera looked at the rainbow. "The sky has gulaal on its face!"

"Exactly," said Nani, and she gently dabbed a little pink on Meera's nose with her fingertip. It wasn't real gulaal, just a pretend boop. "*Holi hai*, little one."

"*Holi hai*, Nani!" Meera booped Nani's nose back.

Slowly the rainbow faded, and the sky turned soft and purple, the colour of sleepy evenings. The frog stopped singing. The peacock folded up his feathers and tucked his head under his wing.

"Even the peacock is going to *so jao*," whispered Nani. *Going to sleep.*

Nani carried Meera to bed and wrapped her in a blanket with little elephants on it. Raindrops on the roof went *tip... tip... tip*, very slow, like a lullaby.

"Nani, will the rainbow come back?"

"Always, *meri gudiya*," said Nani. *My little doll.* "After every rain, the colours come back. And I'll always come back too, to tell you another story."

Meera smiled with her eyes closed. Pink, green, yellow, blue... she counted the colours in her mind, slower and slower...

*Tip... tip...*

*Shubh ratri, Meera. Sweet dreams.*
